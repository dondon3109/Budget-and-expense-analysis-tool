## Design Specificity Verdict

**The product surfaces are authentically authored. The chrome is generic, and the loading subsystem belongs to a different company.**

**Specific — this could not be worn unchanged by another budgeting app:**
- Philippine money reality is structural, not a locale toggle: peso formatting throughout, "e.g. Maya Wallet" placeholder (DashboardPage.tsx:735), a Paste-SMS modal for bank SMS (TransactionsPage.tsx:583-590), "Start with Excel" plus SpreadsheetMigrationWizard. A US or EU budget app does not ship a Paste SMS button.
- The no-bank-connection stance is architecture. "Calculated from your recorded transactions" (DashboardPage.tsx:691 — verified), "Current Ledger Balance" in the adjustment flow (AdjustBalanceModal.tsx:200), a "How these numbers are calculated" disclosure (DashboardPage.tsx:1214-1222), "Zero bank credentials required" on /pricing. This is the hardest claim in the product to make honestly and it is made honestly.
- A real three-voice type system: Newsreader display serif for headings, Manrope for UI, IBM Plex Mono with tabular-nums for numerals (foundation.css:43-46). Not a shadcn default.
- Adjust-balance is modelled as a budgeting decision: it books a real Uncategorized transaction, previews the delta, and offers Undo (AdjustBalanceModal.tsx:194-269).
- Three genuinely designed themes, including a warm-paper "Coffee" palette (foundation.css:215-293). Verified live — dark and coffee are designed palettes, not hue rotations.

**Generic — any SaaS could wear it unchanged:**
- The loading chrome. InlineLoader.css and FullPageLoadingStatus.css describe an "astrolabe gyroscope", "Mini 3D Orbit Satellites", and a "Leading luminous photon cursor" over breathing radial-gradient auras. Tonally that is the opposite of PRODUCT.md's "direct, calm". It is also the loudest, most-seen state in the product.
- The theme-choice gate: a blocking first-run design-system decision.
- The dashboard KPI strip (OverviewStatBar.tsx:51-88) is the default analytics-SaaS card row.
- The date controls are split-brained: MonthSelector is a bespoke, keyboard-perfect month grid, while TransactionFilters uses two raw input[type=date] with no presets (TransactionFilters.tsx:101-118). The authored control exists; the generic one survived where it should have been reused.
- The sidebar (AppShell.tsx:40-58) is a stock icon+label rail whose first item is mislabelled "Profile" for the dashboard route.

### Visual overlays

No user-visible overlay was produced for this run. The detector ran as a CLI scan and the browser pass used a throwaway Playwright harness in /tmp with axe-core injected per page; the Impeccable live-server overlay was not injected into a page. Do not expect highlighted elements in a browser tab.

Two findings from the browser pass that no static scan could reach:
- The FAQ list is invalid HTML: a dl element has details as direct children (definition-list, serious).
- The 404's H1 computes to Manrope 16px/400 — typographically identical to body copy — against Newsreader 58-88px for every other public H1. The page a lost user lands on is the one page with no typographic hierarchy.

## Overall Impression

This is a well-built product wearing some of its least important decisions on its face. The domain work is genuinely good: ledger-vs-bank honesty, the adjust-balance reconciliation loop, the account-deletion dialog, the error-recovery pattern, and a real type system are all things most budgeting apps get wrong, and Zoption gets them right or better.

The problem is the first ninety seconds and the last click. Every public route is gated by two consecutive non-dismissible interstitials before its own content renders — verified on 14 routes, including the 404, whose only recovery link is unreachable behind the modal. That is the entire first impression. And the destructive actions that a ledger most needs to make deliberate are, in three of four implementations, either a native window.confirm, a bare inline "Confirm" next to a pencil icon, or a brand-green primary button labelled "Remove account".

The single biggest opportunity: stop making the user do administration before they can see their money, and make the theme system's own promises true — the chooser says "neutral, low-glare surfaces", then hands dark-mode users near-white panels because two CSS variables it references were never defined.

## What's Working

1. **AccountDeletionDialog is the best surface in the codebase.** canSubmit requires both password and a typed DELETE (AccountDeletionDialog.tsx:34), consequences are enumerated explicitly (:116-119), progress is announced with aria-busy and role=status aria-live (:115, :158), focus is trapped and restored via useRootLock (:36, :63-84), and the confirm is .button danger (:166). An irreversible act made legible and slow without being obstructive — exactly the brand PRODUCT.md describes.
2. **The error-recovery pattern is uniform and honest.** Every failed panel renders a bold human title, the underlying message, and a retry (TransactionsPage.tsx:485-493; BudgetsPage.tsx:155-163; FinancialPlanPage.tsx:300-310), and the Android release fetch degrades to a validated bundled snapshot rather than a dead link (useAndroidRelease.ts:29-30). "Report failure honestly" is implemented, not just written down. Browser confirmation: with the API fully down, /api/reviews 502'd and the section simply did not render — no error text, no broken layout, no unresolved spinner.
3. **MonthSelector is a genuinely authored interaction.** Full grid keyboard navigation — Arrow, PageUp, PageDown, Home, End — with bounds-aware focus movement (MonthSelector.tsx:113-165), Escape and focus restore (:92-95), aria-haspopup and aria-expanded (:173-176), roving tabindex with aria-pressed (:231).
4. **The type and token system has real discipline.** Three typefaces with distinct jobs, tabular numerals on amounts (private-primitives.css:176-182), tokenised income/expense colour, right-aligned amount columns, explicit +/- signs, and centralised money formatting. The owner's stated dislike of gradients on cards and backgrounds is honoured: six gradient declarations exist in the whole app, none on a card, floating card, or page background, and the browser reports zero computed gradient background-images on the rendered landing page.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Two non-dismissible interstitials gate every public route
- **What:** ThemeChoiceDialog opens whenever no stored preference exists (ThemeChoiceDialog.tsx:45), explicitly swallows Escape (":101-106" — event.preventDefault plus stopPropagation), and ships no close control; its only buttons are three theme radios plus "Confirm Light theme". CookieConsentBanner is gated behind it (CookieConsentBanner.tsx:13-20) over a fixed full-screen backdrop (cookieConsent.css:1-3).
- **Why it matters:** Browser-verified on /, /pricing, /faq, /guides, /tutorials, /changelog, /login, /signup, /install, /thank-you, all three legal pages, and /does-not-exist: the dialog persists after Escape, persists after a backdrop click, and localStorage stays empty. A first-time visitor to any link a friend shared cannot read the page. The 404's only recovery affordance is unreachable. Clearing site data re-enters the gate. Every new user's first and only experience is a design-system decision followed by a cookie decision. This is also a peak-end violation: the remembered end of the first run is paperwork.
- **Fix:** Make the theme gate dismissible (X and Escape both resolve to a persisted default) and move the real choice to /app/settings or a post-signup step. Convert the cookie banner to a non-modal bottom bar with no backdrop so no page is ever unreachable. Stop rendering the dialog's h1 on content pages — it currently produces two h1s per page.
- **Command:** onboard

### [P0] Destructive actions speak three languages; two of them are dangerous
- **What:** (a) native window.confirm for goals, debts and subscriptions — verified at FinancialPlanPage.tsx:255, :261 and SubscriptionsPage.tsx:239; (b) "Remove account" rendered as .button primary, i.e. brand green, verified at DashboardPage.tsx:1116, even though .button.danger exists (foundation.css:622); (c) an unstyled inline Confirm/Cancel for transaction delete with no undo (TransactionTable.tsx:156-171).
- **Why it matters:** window.confirm is unthemed, blocks the JS thread, ignores the app's focus management, and can be suppressed by the browser — the user may never see the prompt. A green "Remove account" teaches people that the brand colour means "safe to proceed", which is backwards for a ledger; nothing warns that the overall balance drops by that account's amount. A bare "Confirm" one row-height from a pencil icon deletes a financial record in a single click with no reversibility. This contradicts PRODUCT.md's own commitment to being "explicit about the result of sensitive actions" in the three places it matters most.
- **Fix:** One shared ConfirmDialog modelled on AccountDeletionDialog — danger button, one explicit consequence sentence, useRootLock, Tab trap, focus restore — routed through all four destructive flows. Add a five-second undo on transaction delete: the optimistic snapshot machinery already exists (restoreOptimisticSnapshot, TransactionsPage.tsx:241) and is currently used only for rollback-on-error. There is no toast system in the app (verified — zero toast primitives), so the undo affordance needs a small one.
- **Command:** harden

### [P1] Colour and theme tokens are broken in two independent ways
- **What:** (a) The muted text token fails AA. --muted: #6c716c (foundation.css:55) on --page #f4f1e9 computes to **4.41:1**, below the 4.5:1 floor — verified by independent calculation. It is the single source of recurring contrast failures across 13 of 14 public routes, and accounts for 63 failing nodes on /privacy-policy alone, plus .legal-footer-meta p, .legal-footer-version, .legal-summary, .legal-updated and .breadcrumb-link. Note --subtle: #66706a computes to 4.55:1 and passes, so the "muted" token is lighter than the "subtle" token — an inverted pair. Separately, --expense #d96b35 on white is 3.44:1 and used for .extracted-amount at 13.5px bold.
  (b) Two CSS variables are referenced 13 times and defined **zero** times — verified by full-repo grep: --surface-muted (11 usages) and --subtle-border (2 usages). Most supply a hardcoded cool-grey fallback (#f9fafb, #f3f4f6, #e5e7eb), so dark mode renders near-white panels on a #0f1115 page while the theme chooser promises "Neutral, low-glare surfaces". **Orchestrator addition, worse than first reported:** four usages have no fallback at all — SpreadsheetMigrationWizard.css:45, :250, :270, :311 use bare var(--surface-muted), which makes the declaration invalid and drops it entirely, so those surfaces get no background rather than a wrong one.
- **Why it matters:** The contrast failure is not a taste issue; it is 13 of 14 public routes failing WCAG AA on the body-secondary text that carries legal, pricing and breadcrumb copy. The undefined variables make the product's most confident promise — a genuinely designed three-theme system — false in exactly the mode the user chose it for, and inject cool Tailwind greys into a deliberately warm palette in light and coffee modes.
- **Fix:** Darken --muted to roughly --subtle's value (4.55:1) and swap the inverted naming so "subtle" is the lighter of the two; darken --expense for small text or reserve it for large/UI use. Define --surface-muted and --subtle-border per theme in foundation.css under :root, [data-theme="dark"] and [data-theme="coffee"], mapped to --surface-soft and --line, then delete every fallback so the bug cannot silently return.
- **Command:** colorize

### [P1] No power-user path: no shortcuts, no bulk actions, no saved views
- **What:** Zero hits for metaKey or ctrlKey across apps/web/src — there is not one keyboard shortcut in the product. TransactionTable has no selection column and TransactionsPage passes only single-row handlers (TransactionsPage.tsx:503-513). Zero saved views. Filters live in component state (:111-114) while sort is persisted to localStorage (:139-151) — an arbitrary split. Pagination is page-size 10 with prev/next only and no "showing 1-10 of N" range control.
- **Why it matters:** Re-categorising twenty rows after an import means twenty modal round-trips. The capability already exists inside the product — ImportPage.tsx:1103-1129 does cross-page bulk selection with a bulk kind/category toolbar — so the ledger looks like an oversight rather than a scope decision. For a data-heavy budgeting tool this is the difference between a tool and a chore, and the predictable outcome is that Alex exports to a spreadsheet to do bulk edits, which is the exact behaviour Zoption exists to replace.
- **Fix:** Add a selection column and sticky selection toolbar to TransactionTable, reusing the existing .bulk-category-toolbar pattern from ImportPage. Add / to focus search, n for new transaction, Cmd/Ctrl+Enter to submit TransactionForm, and j/k row movement. Move filters into the URL so a view is shareable and reload-safe, then layer named saved views on top. Add date presets (This month, Last 30 days, Year to date) to TransactionFilters — the most common date question currently has no one-click answer.
- **Command:** optimize

### [P1] Modal focus management is inconsistent, including on the most-used dialog
- **What:** 13 files declare aria-modal but implement no Tab trap — AdjustBalanceModal, AssistantMemoryPanel, ShareBudgetModal, CalendarEventForm, SpreadsheetMigrationWizard, DebtForm, FinancialGoalForm, CancellationGuideDrawer, SubscriptionForm, SupportChat, SmsQuickPasteModal, TransactionVoiceEntry, and **TransactionForm**, the most-used modal in the app (TransactionForm.tsx:267-272). Eleven other files do trap correctly. Escape is missing entirely in AdjustBalanceModal, SmsQuickPasteModal, TransactionVoiceEntry and AdminProviderConfigsPage. The shell's Edit and Remove account modals (DashboardPage.tsx:881, :1085) have neither Escape nor a trap.
- **Why it matters:** Tab from the last control in Add Transaction lands on the page behind the modal — a screen-reader and keyboard user can operate controls they cannot see, and the focus ring disappears into inert content. The app already solved this well in eleven places, so this is inconsistency, not missing capability. Verified as well-built and worth keeping: useRootLock is refcounted, saves and restores prior body overflow and #root inert plus aria-hidden, and is consumed by 12 files.
- **Fix:** Route every aria-modal dialog through useRootLock and one shared focus-trap helper rather than hand-rolling per component; add Escape to the four outliers. Replace the .transaction-form input:focus override (TransactionForm.css:220-221, border-color #76a994 plus a 12%-alpha shadow, roughly 2.5:1 — below the 3:1 WCAG 1.4.11 floor for UI components) with the global 3px solid var(--focus) ring already defined at foundation.css:324-332.
- **Command:** audit

### [P2] Mobile is where this product is most used and least finished
- **What:** .dashboard-account-actions button is fixed at 30x30 (DashboardPage.css:1198-1200) and none of the four media queries override it, so Adjust, Edit and **Remove** sit as three 30px icons in a tight row on a phone. The same pattern is correctly fixed to 44x44 on TransactionsPage at 760px (TransactionsPage.css:505-508); the dashboard simply missed it, on the delete button. Also sub-44px: .icon-button.compact 36x36 (private-primitives.css:293-298), .text-button 32px, .refresh-button 36px, .table-status button 36px. Measured live at 390x844: 35 sub-44px targets on the landing page. On /pricing the mobile header wraps its own labels — "Sign in" and "Start free" each render on two lines inside 44px controls. Public mobile navigation is inconsistent or absent: no menu trigger at all on /pricing, /install, /faq or /changelog, and desktop header link counts range from 13 (/) to 1 (/faq, /guides, /changelog) to 0 (/login, /signup, /tutorials, /does-not-exist). The 404 ships no header, no footer, no nav and one CTA. The floating support-chat launcher overlaps trust-strip text on / at 390px. No page-level horizontal overflow was detected on any route (390/390 on all seven tested) — the only wide elements are inside a deliberate horizontal scroll container.
- **Why it matters:** Mis-taps on a financial record are not recoverable, and the app already knows the correct pattern — it just applied it to one page and not the other. A FAQ page whose header offers no way to navigate anywhere is a dead end for exactly the user who is already confused.
- **Fix:** Add the missing 760px block for .dashboard-account-actions button at 44x44, matching TransactionsPage. Raise .icon-button.compact and .text-button to 44 on touch. Give the public header one consistent component with a menu trigger on every route and stop the label wrapping. Give the 404 the standard shell and a real display-size H1.
- **Command:** adapt

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (power user) — the app actively slows him down.** Zero keyboard shortcuts anywhere, so every entry is sidebar, scan a 90px header for the right one of five buttons, modal, eight fields, submit. No multi-select on the ledger, so post-import cleanup is one modal per row, while ImportPage already proves cross-page bulk edit was built. Pagination is page-size 10 with prev/next only and no range control. Filters are not in the URL and not persisted, so he cannot bookmark a view or open one in a second tab. He will export to a spreadsheet.

**Sam (accessibility-dependent) — several concrete failures.** The Add Transaction modal declares aria-modal but never locks the root or traps Tab, so focus escapes to the page behind. Its focus ring is overridden to roughly 2.5:1, below the 3:1 floor for UI components. The muted token fails AA on 13 of 14 public routes, so legal, pricing and breadcrumb secondary text is under-contrast everywhere. Dark mode delivers near-white panels because two variables are undefined. The theme dialog puts a second h1 on every page. Dashboard destructive controls are 30x30. Credit where due: alt text is complete (3 img, 0 missing), there are zero unlabeled icon-only controls across 387 buttons and 173 anchors, and reduced motion is globally handled for transitions (foundation.css:921-935) with 13 files also declaring their own blocks.

**Casey (distracted mobile) — the shell is right, the contents are not.** First mobile visit is a theme bottom-sheet, then a cookie dialog, then content — the same on a 404. 35 sub-44px targets on the landing page, with a 30px Remove-account button the worst of them. On /pricing the header buttons wrap their own labels. Assistant, Import, Goals and debt, Subscriptions, Settings and Tutorials are all two taps deep in the More sheet. Credit: 52px mobile tab items, safe-area insets, a drawer with Escape, backdrop dismissal and body scroll lock, and a dedicated 760px layout for row actions.

## Minor Observations

- **No error boundary exists anywhere in apps/web** (zero hits for ErrorBoundary, componentDidCatch, getDerivedStateFromError). Any render error in any route blanks the entire authenticated app to a white screen with no recovery path. For a product whose stated principle is reporting failure honestly, this is the one failure mode with no designed response. Worth pairing with the harden pass.
- The FAQ is a 34-line flat list of 14 always-expanded Q&A blocks (FaqPage.tsx:14-22) — roughly 3227px tall on desktop, no accordion, no anchors, no search, no categories. It is the one public page that is not designed. It also stacks two back-navigation affordances: a breadcrumb plus a separate "Back to Zoption" link.
- 54 of 76 th elements have no scope attribute, including all six in TransactionTable (TransactionTable.tsx:47, :64, :81-83, :101) and three in the public comparison table (LandingPage.tsx:1425-1427). Only 3 tables have a caption. Not an axe violation, since browsers infer headers in simple tables, but a real screen-reader quality gap in the app's primary data surface.
- 687 hardcoded hex values across 30 component stylesheets, bypassing the token system. Worst offender by palette drift: SmsQuickPasteModal.css is built from Tailwind slate and blue (#e2e8f0, #64748b, #1e293b, #2563eb) inside a warm-paper green system, including a blue-600 primary button.
- Raw enum values leak into the UI: account.type renders lowercase "checking"/"credit"/"cash" (DashboardPage.tsx:784) while the same concepts are title-cased everywhere else. The Type badge prints plural "Expenses" for a single row (TransactionTable.tsx:147) while the filter says "Money out".
- The Assistant page has no visible heading — it is the only authenticated route with an sr-only h1 (AssistantPage.tsx:392), and the one where orientation matters most.
- Breadcrumbs are built and barely used: seven public surfaces, zero authenticated pages, including the nested /app/admin/* routes where they would help most.
- Two clickable areas are keyboard-inoperable: the SpreadsheetMigrationWizard dropzone (":346") has no role, tabIndex or key handler, so keyboard users cannot open the file picker, and three landing-page elements have role and tabIndex but no onKeyDown (LandingPage.tsx:1055, :1069, :1083) so they are not Enter/Space operable. One text input in the wizard (":429") is labelled only by its placeholder.
- Pulsing text: .inline-loader-label animates opacity 0.7 to 1 indefinitely (InlineLoader.css:208-210, :291-299, :322-324). Continuous text opacity animation is a legibility cost for low-vision and dyslexic users and carries no information.
- No unsaved-changes guard anywhere (zero hits for beforeunload or useBlocker). A user editing a long budget plan who clicks a sidebar link loses the draft silently.
- One motion gap: CancellationGuideDrawer.css:9 and :22 declare bare animation properties and the file has no reduced-motion block, so its slide-in escapes the global transition reset. Also AdminCustomerReviewsPage.css:395 removes the outline and the file's focus-visible rules target other selectors.
- The dashboard nests an "Account management" region inside the "Overall balance" region (DashboardPage.tsx:670, :696), which produces a confusing landmark hierarchy for screen-reader landmark navigation.
- The landing 4-up feature row has misaligned titles: cards 1 and 2 carry a "TOP FEATURE" pill above the title and cards 3 and 4 do not, so titles sit about 29px lower in the first two. The mobile trust pills ("Voice Entry.", "Scan Receipt", "AI Assistant · 100% Private") each wrap to two lines inside their pill.
- /login on mobile leaves roughly 160px of dead space between the sign-in button and the "New to Zoption?" prompt.
- Pagination, loading and empty states are otherwise a strength worth preserving: three genuinely specific empty states (first-run workspace onboarding with two named paths, DashboardPage.tsx:1137-1176; "No transactions in month", CalendarPage.tsx:486-489; "Add your first transaction", ":461-482").
- The ledger-versus-live-bank distinction is handled better than most products that make this claim. The one gap: the reconciliation loop that keeps the ledger honest is under-promoted at first run — "Adjust balance" is a small ghost button in the account card (DashboardPage.tsx:700-708), and the empty state offers "Bring your data" or "Add manually" (:1147-1159) but never "tell us what you already have".

## Questions to Consider

1. If balances come from recorded ledger entries with no opening snapshot, why is "Adjust balance" a ghost button buried in the account card rather than the primary first-run action? The first thing anyone does with a new budgeting tool is enter what is actually in their account.
2. The app bulk-categorises imported rows across pages (ImportPage.tsx:1103-1129) but cannot bulk-edit ten rows already in the ledger. Deliberate scope cut, or two features built by different instincts?
3. The theme chooser is the most carefully engineered dialog in the product — live previews, roving tabindex, keyboard grid, focus trap. Deleting a transaction is two unstyled text buttons. Which one is Zoption?
4. When the network is down the user reads a raw error.message (DashboardPage.tsx:511). PRODUCT.md promises to report failure honestly — is an unedited exception string honest, or just unedited?
5. What is the intended first impression for someone who clicked a link from a friend? Right now it is a theme decision followed by a cookie decision, and the page they came to read is behind both.
